## Questionnaire Snapshots

The Iteration 3 presentation contains an **18-response** snapshot. The final report and individual website screenshots contain **19 responses**. The new README and case study consistently use the 19-response results. The available files do not establish the exact update history, so the difference is recorded rather than explained as a confirmed additional participant.

The 37-response technology/privacy questionnaire is a separate study. Its percentages should not be combined with the later support-needs survey. Multiple-selection questions can total more than 100%.

The later questionnaire also asks about summaries in different contexts: 63.2% for one summary-related question and 57.9% for a later concise-summary option. They represent different questions, rather than interchangeable results.

## Object-Study Sample

The personal portfolio describes twelve planned participants, eight actual participants, and one person declining to interact with the available objects. Seven usable records informed the reported preference analysis. The final report describes the analysed sample as seven participants.

The documents therefore distinguish eight participants from seven usable sessions. Five of seven selected a squeezable object first; all seven included one in their top three. Existing objects differed in material, shape, size, appearance, and interaction. The study does not isolate the causal effect of squeezing and is not an evaluation of a functioning SqueezeCare device.

## Final Scope and Original Graphics

The final report narrows the proposal to emotional support and communication assistance. Some exhibition graphics retain broader wording about decision support, GP booking, emergency calls, or protected data. Those graphics are preserved as historical project material. Their language is not used to claim implemented capabilities.

AI-assisted symptom summarisation and translation remain proposed. Diagnosis, clinical urgency assessment, and automated medical decisions are outside the scope used in the new documents. No translation engine, firmware, connected service, haptic mapping, privacy control, or security implementation is demonstrated by the materials.

Light feedback appears both as a proposed requirement and as a possible future direction. Its exact role was not settled. Community or anonymous support similarly requires further social and privacy design.

## Limits of the Evidence

The studies use small, exploratory samples and self-reported preferences. The object comparison involves imagined situations, rather than documented use during illness. No causal reduction in stress, clinical improvement, reliable translation, or successful deployment was measured.

The case study summarises the supplied research and does not independently verify every literature citation. Biological or therapeutic claims in the source literature are not treated as proven effects of SqueezeCare. Future activities in the case study are proposed next steps, not completed project milestones.
